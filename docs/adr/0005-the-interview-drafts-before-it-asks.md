# 5. The interview drafts before it asks

**Status:** accepted, 2 November 2026

## Context

The results depend on a few company facts: what the company sells, who buys it, and the category an organiser will put the user in. That category decides the price they pay and whether they are let in at all. Some people have those facts to hand. Many can answer for their own job but not for the company, and a blank question at the start of a run is where they give up.

## Decision

The interview opens with "What's your company website? Or describe what you do in a line." With a website, the skill reads the homepage and up to 3 more pages from the same site, then drafts the company answers for the user to correct. Later questions come with a draft wherever one can be made. "Not sure" is always accepted: the skill uses a sensible default and marks it as an assumption in the brief.

Who the user wants to meet is always confirmed out loud, never taken silently from the website.

## Why

- Correcting a draft is quicker than writing an answer, and people who would stall still get a good brief.
- Most people do not know that conferences price by category until they are shown the vendor rate. Drafting the category catches it for them.
- A website shows who a company markets to, which is not always who its people want to meet. That answer counts double in scoring, so it gets an explicit yes.
- No LinkedIn pages, enrichment tools or whole-site crawls: they add failure points for little gain.
