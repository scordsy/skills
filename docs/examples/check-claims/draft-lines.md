[S1] # Why B2B Buying Committees Keep Growing, and What Marketers Should Do About It
[S2] If your deals feel slower and more crowded than they did five years ago, you are not imagining it.
[S3] The single decision-maker has become a rarity in B2B. In their place sits a committee, and that committee keeps adding seats.
[S4] ## The numbers behind the crowd
[S5] The trend has been building for a decade.
[S6] CEB (now part of Gartner) put the average buying group at 5.4 people in 2015, and by 2017 its research in Harvard Business Review had raised that to 6.8.
[S7] Gartner now describes a typical complex B2B purchase as involving six to ten decision-makers, each arriving with four or five pieces of information they have gathered on their own.
[S8] More recent studies put the figure higher still.
[S9] Forrester's 2024 State of Business Buying report found that the average purchase involves 13 people, and that 89% of purchases span two or more departments.
[S10] 6sense's Buyer Experience research puts the typical buying group at around 11 members.
[S11] These groups also do most of their work before a vendor is involved.
[S12] 6sense found that buyers are roughly 70% of the way through their process before they speak to a seller, and that the vendor they contact first wins the deal about four times out of five.
[S13] Gartner's research shows buyers spend only 17% of their purchase time meeting potential suppliers, and that is split across every vendor under consideration.
[S14] ## Why committees are getting bigger
[S15] **Software touches everyone now.**
[S16] A purchase that once belonged to IT now affects finance, operations, security, legal and the teams who use the product every day.
[S17] Each one wants a say.
[S18] **Risk and compliance have moved up the agenda.**
[S19] Data privacy rules, security reviews and, more recently, AI governance have added reviewers who can stop a deal even if they will never use the product.
[S20] **Budgets are under closer scrutiny.**
[S21] Since the 2022 downturn, many companies have pushed spending decisions up to the CFO's office.
[S22] Procurement and finance now appear in deals that a department head used to sign off alone.
[S23] **Products spread from the bottom up.**
[S24] Slack, Zoom and Atlassian grew because individual teams adopted them first.
[S25] When usage spreads and an enterprise contract follows, the people who chose the tool are joined by IT, security and procurement.
[S26] The users become one voice among many.
[S27] ## What it costs when you ignore it
[S28] Bigger groups are harder to get to agreement.
[S29] Forrester found that 86% of B2B purchases stall at some point in the process, and that 81% of buyers end up dissatisfied with the provider they chose.
[S30] Often the real competitor is not another vendor.
[S31] It is the committee failing to agree and doing nothing.
[S32] ## What this means for marketers
[S33] **Stop measuring individuals and start measuring groups.**
[S34] The classic lead model scores one person at a time.
[S35] A webinar attendee from finance and a whitepaper download from IT at the same company look like two weak leads when they are one strong signal.
[S36] Forrester has argued for years that buying groups should replace the marketing-qualified lead, and vendors have followed.
[S37] Adobe launched Journey Optimizer B2B Edition in 2024 around buying groups specifically, and Palo Alto Networks has spoken publicly about piloting buying-group processes and seeing better conversion as a result.
[S38] **Write for every seat at the table.**
[S39] The CFO wants a payback period.
[S40] The security lead wants your certifications and architecture.
[S41] The end user wants to know it will not make their day harder.
[S42] One generic ebook serves none of them.
[S43] Map your content to roles and find the gaps.
[S44] **Equip your champion.**
[S45] Most of the selling happens in rooms you are not in.
[S46] Give your internal advocate material they can forward: a one-page business case, a security summary, a short comparison.
[S47] Snowflake and Salesforce both invest heavily in ROI calculators and business-value tools for this reason.
[S48] **Get on the shortlist early.**
[S49] If buyers are 70% decided before they call, brand and category awareness are not soft metrics.
[S50] They decide whether you are considered at all.
[S51] **Help the group agree.**
[S52] Content that helps a committee align, such as buying guides, evaluation checklists and stakeholder FAQs, can be worth more than another product page.
[S53] ## The takeaway
[S54] Buying committees are not going back to being small.
[S55] The marketers who do well will be the ones who stop asking "who is the lead?" and start asking "who is in the room, and what does each of them need to say yes?"
