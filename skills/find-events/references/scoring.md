# Scoring

Score every record that is open to the user, checked or not. The weights are the same for every run. The brief decides what each dimension means for this person.

## The five dimensions

| Code | Dimension | Range | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| R | Right people | 0 to 3 | How much of the room is the people the brief says they want to meet |
| A | Conversation access | 0 to 3 | Whether they can actually talk to those people there |
| V | Value for money | 0 to 3 | The whole cost (their ticket price, travel, nights away) against their budget and the conversations they can expect |
| L | Learning | 0 to 2 | Whether they will get sharper at their craft or their market by being there |
| B | Bonus | 0 to 1 | Anything else the brief cares about that the other four miss |

**Score = 2R + A + V + L + B, out of 15.** Right people counts double because being in the wrong room fails the whole trip, whatever else is true.

## What each score means

**R, right people**
- 3: most of the room is the people they want to meet
- 2: a solid share of the room, or a room of only the second group the brief names
- 1: some are there, but outnumbered by others (vendors, agencies, founders, the wrong sector)
- 0: hardly any

**A, conversation access**
- 3: built for talking: a meetup, roundtable or small room, or a matchmaking programme their category may use
- 2: real networking time, an attendee app with messaging, or an expo floor the right people walk
- 1: mostly stage and audience, with breaks
- 0: no realistic way to talk to them, for example a matchmaking programme closed to their category

**V, value for money**, using the record's **Your price** line as written, not the listed price
- 3: free or close to it, within their normal travel
- 2: a sensible share of their budget for the conversations expected
- 1: expensive for what it offers, or a long trip for a thin room
- 0: more than their budget allows, or poor value at their price

**L, learning**
- 2: sessions they would actively learn from
- 1: some useful content
- 0: nothing relevant

**B, bonus**
- 1: something the brief cares about that the rest misses: material for content, partners or referrals, a trip it can be combined with
- 0: nothing extra

## Tiers

| Tier | Score | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Book | 12 or more | Worth the diary. Register or buy early. |
| Consider | 9 to 11 | Go if it is cheap, close, or combines with another trip. |
| Skip | below 9 | Only with a reason the score misses. |

## Rules

- **Dead and unverifiable events are not scored.** They go at the bottom of the shortlist.
- **An event whose gate keeps the user out is not scored as bookable.** It goes at the bottom too, with the gate stated. That includes an event they can only enter by sponsoring or exhibiting: say which route, and whether its price is published. Sponsoring a room is a different decision from buying a ticket.
- **When the record's Your price says "not published"**, score value for money at 1 at most, and say "ask the price first" wherever the event appears.
- **A sold-out event is not scored.** It goes at the bottom, as sold out.
- **An event whose next edition falls outside the date window is not scored** and is left off the shortlist.
- **Pattern-estimates are scored**, and marked "(est)" with "confirm the date before booking travel".
- **Where value for money needs a currency conversion**, use a rough rate and say so in the shortlist. Never write a converted price into a record.
- **Write each score as its parts**, for example `13 (R3 A3 V2 L1 B1)`, so the person can see why.
- When two events tie, the cheaper one for the user goes first. An unpublished price counts as dearer than any published one.
