# Reader instructions

You are reading a call transcript to list what a CRM record of this call depends on. You will not see any summary of the call. That is deliberate: a summary read first decides what seems to matter, and the thing it left out starts to look unimportant. Read the transcript cold.

Read only the numbered transcript you were given. Write in the format in `formats.md`, then reply as your instructions say.

## Key facts

List every fact of these seven kinds, in transcript order, one fact per block:

| Kind | What counts |
|---|---|
| `next-step` | Something someone will do after the call. Name the owner and any date in the title: "Ben sends the pricing by Friday". |
| `commitment` | Something either side promised or agreed on the call, that is not a task: "Dan agreed to a pilot with one team". |
| `date` | A date or deadline that matters to the deal: renewal, go-live, budget cycle, a decision date. |
| `number` | A figure that matters: budget, price, discount, headcount, volume, contract length. |
| `who-decides` | Who makes, signs off or can block the decision, and any threshold. |
| `objection` | A concern, blocker, risk or reason not to buy, as the speaker put it. |
| `competitor` | Another vendor, product, in-house build or "do nothing" named as an option. |

- **Quote every fact.** Copy the words exactly, as `formats.md` says. A fact you cannot quote is not a key fact: you are listing what was said, not what you conclude.
- **Say it as plainly as it was said.** If the speaker hedged ("we might", "probably", "I'd hope"), the title keeps the hedge. "Dan might bring in procurement", not "Dan brings in procurement".
- **Record who said they would do it.** The owner of a next step is the person whose line it is, by its label. If that label is one you list below as doubtful, still name the label's owner and let the doubtful label speak for itself.
- **Skip the small talk,** and anything that would not change what someone does next on this deal or account.
- **One fact per block.** A line holding a date and a number is two blocks.

Be complete on these seven kinds and sparing outside them. A long list of minor facts hides the one that matters.

## Doubtful labels

Call recorders mix up who said what, more often with three or more people on the call. You cannot hear the audio, so you can never know who really spoke. What you can see is a line whose words argue with its label. Flag those, and only those. Signs:

- someone answering their own question, or two lines from the same label that read as a question and its answer;
- a speaker addressed by name in their own line ("Thanks, Priya" from Priya);
- "our product", "our team" or "we" said from the wrong side of the table, against what that person says elsewhere;
- a line that only makes sense as the reply of someone else.

For each, give the line, its label, who it more likely was (or `unclear`), and why in one sentence. Never change the label, and never write a key fact as if the label were different.

A transcript with no doubtful labels is normal. Inventing one to look thorough is as bad as missing one.
