# Re-check instructions

If you cannot search the web and read pages in this conversation, say so and stop. A check from memory is worse than no check.

You did not trace these claims. Something else did, and you are the check on its work. That separation is the point of the method: anything checking its own work looks in the same place and confirms what it found first. Read this as an instruction to be difficult.

## What you are given

Some claims from a draft. For each one: the claim, the draft's exact words, the source the draft gives (if any), and the verdict the first pass reached.

You are not given the first publisher the first pass named, its evidence, its reasons or its problem types, on purpose. Find your own.

## What to do

**Treat each claim as wrong until a first publisher's page says otherwise.**

For every claim, trace it yourself from the start, as `research.md` sets out:

1. Find the first publisher: whoever produced the figure or made the statement, on their own page. Go past every page that repeats it.
2. Read that page and copy the exact words.
3. Look for anything newer from the same publisher on the same thing: a later edition, a later announcement, a changed name or owner.
4. Reach your own verdict and your own problem types.

Most claims you are given were called `supported`. A wrong "supported" is the costly error, because it looks finished. The commonest ways one is wrong:

- **The page it rests on repeats the figure** and is not the first publisher.
- **The publisher has since replaced the figure,** and nobody looked for the newer one.
- **The figure is right and the credit is wrong:** the wrong vendor, the wrong year, the wrong edition.
- **Who the figure counts has been lost** on the way into the draft.
- **A story's plain core is on the page and its colourful detail is not.**

Some claims reached you with another verdict because a script could not confirm the first pass's quote. Check those the same way. The verdict you were given is a claim about the claim, not a fact.

## Sources

`research.md` gives the sources, the tools, the four verdicts, the ten problem types and how to tell neighbouring types apart. The rules are the same for you: the first publisher's own page is the only evidence, quotes are copied exactly, a page that refuses is left alone, and nothing is guessed.

## What to write

One record for every claim you were given, in the record format (`record-format.md`), with your own verdict, problems, first publisher, date, finding and evidence. Every rule for a record applies to yours, including the Use instead line on a `differs` and on a `supported` claim that carries a problem type. Number your evidence `E1`, `E2` and so on.

Add one line to each record, after Finding:

```
- Check notes: <what you changed and why, or "agreed: <the deciding evidence in a few words>">
```

- **Change a verdict only on evidence.** Your record replaces the first pass's in the report, so it must stand alone.
- **If you agree,** still write the full record from what you read yourself.
- **If you cannot find a first publisher,** the verdict is `no-source`, with your searches.
- **If you find one and cannot read its page,** the verdict is `could-not-open`, with what stood in the way and what a person should look for.
- **Inventing a problem to look thorough is the same failure as missing one.**

Reply with one line only: how many claims you checked, how many you agreed with, and how many you changed.
