# The six prompts

Written on 25 November 2026, before any draft existed. Each is what a working marketer or seller might type. None asks the writer to cite or verify anything. Paste each one, exactly, into a fresh chat to run the same test on another assistant.

The two seller names, Tallybridge and Harbourlight, are made up for this test. Any match with a real company is an accident.

## A1: article

```text
Write a 700-word blog post for our company blog on why B2B buying committees are getting bigger and what that means for marketers. Include recent statistics and examples from well-known companies.
```

## A2: article

```text
Write a 600-word LinkedIn article on the state of AI adoption in B2B sales teams in 2026. Use data from industry reports and name companies that are doing it well.
```

## A3: article

```text
Write an 800-word article on why cold email reply rates are falling and what top-performing teams do differently. Back it up with numbers and real company examples.
```

## A4: article

```text
Write a 700-word blog post on account-based marketing ROI: is ABM worth it for mid-market SaaS companies? Include stats and case studies from named companies.
```

## E1: sales email

```text
I work at Tallybridge, which makes software that automates customer onboarding for B2B SaaS companies. Write a cold email to the VP of Customer Success at Gong. Mention something recent about Gong and include one or two industry statistics that make the case. Keep it under 150 words.
```

## E2: sales email

```text
I sell cybersecurity awareness training to mid-sized UK businesses. My company is called Harbourlight. Write a cold email to the IT Director at Greggs. Reference recent company news and a relevant statistic about cyber attacks on UK businesses. Keep it under 150 words.
```
