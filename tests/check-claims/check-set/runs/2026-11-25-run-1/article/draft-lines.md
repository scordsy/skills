[S1] # Selling to small UK businesses in 2027: the facts behind the pitch
[S2] Most advice on selling to small businesses is opinion dressed as data.
[S3] Here are the numbers we keep seeing in pitches, and the stories that travel with them.
[S4] ## The market is big, and easy to join
[S5] At the start of 2026 there were 5.6 million private sector businesses in the UK, according to the government's business population estimates.
[S6] Starting one is cheap: it costs £50 to register a company online at Companies House.
[S7] That is a lot of buyers, and most of them have no procurement team.
[S8] ## What keeps owners awake
[S9] Security is the first worry.
[S10] The government's Cyber Security Breaches Survey 2025/2026 found that 53% of businesses had suffered a cyber breach or attack in the previous 12 months.
[S11] The same survey found that 40% of businesses reported their most disruptive breach outside their organisation.
[S12] Cash is the second.
[S13] The law now caps payment terms between businesses at 60 days.
[S14] The government also intends to give every commercial contract a right to statutory interest at 8% above the Bank of England base rate, according to the law firm Mayer Brown (https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/uk-government-response-to-late-payment-consultation).
[S15] If you sell on 90-day terms, plan for that now.
[S16] ## Deals stall, and AI is not a cure yet
[S17] Forrester's State of Business Buying 2024 report found that 86% of B2B purchases stall during the buying process.
[S18] Vendors say AI is the answer.
[S19] Salesforce's research found that teams that deeply use AI generate 77% more revenue per rep.
[S20] Gong, which sells that kind of software, said in March 2025 that it had passed $300 million in annual recurring revenue.
[S21] Its State of Revenue AI 2026 report says the top 5% of AI implementations are owned by revenue operations rather than IT.
[S22] Be careful with email all the same.
[S23] Google says Gmail's defences stop up to 99.9% of spam, phishing and malware from reaching inboxes.
[S24] One figure doing the rounds says 64% of UK finance directors delete any cold email longer than 90 words.
[S25] ## Do the unscalable thing
[S26] Paul Graham put it bluntly in his essay "Do Things that Don't Scale": "The most important thing founders have to do at the start is to recruit users manually."
[S27] He went on: "You can't wait for users to come to you.
[S28] You have to go out and get them."
[S29] Stripe is his example.
[S30] When someone agreed to try Stripe, the Collison brothers would say "Right then, give me your laptop" and set them up on the spot, a technique Y Combinator calls a "Collison installation", and they signed their first 50 customers that way.
[S31] ## The tools get bought
[S32] The software your buyers use keeps changing hands.
[S33] Salesforce agreed to buy Slack at an enterprise value of approximately $27.7 billion (https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2020/12/01/salesforce-signs-definitive-agreement-to-acquire-slack/).
[S34] Intuit completed its purchase of Mailchimp on 1 November 2021.
[S35] Plan your integrations around the owner, not the logo.
[S36] ## One last case study
[S37] Account-based advertising can work at this end of the market too.
[S38] RollWorks' case study on PayScale reports a 500% increase in traffic from target accounts.
[S39] Start small, check your facts, and pick up the phone.
